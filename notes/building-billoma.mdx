Billoma looks like invoicing. It is actually a problem of continuity. Operators already know how to make a PDF. They do not know how to keep that PDF, the payment link, the reminder and the reply in one object as the week goes on.

## The product is the lifecycle

Draft, send, view, remind, pay, settle. Every feature that does not sit on that line is a distraction. I cut a lot of “nice for a billing suite” ideas because they would have created a second status that could disagree with the first.

## Payments will lie to you

Webhooks arrive twice. Clients refresh. Users click pay, panic, click again. If your charge identity is “the invoice id,” you will double-collect. If your charge identity is an idempotency key you control, you will sleep.

## Messages belong on the invoice

Email is not a system of record. When a customer asks “did you get this?” the answer has to be in the same thread as the money. That forced a design I liked: delivery is an event, not a side effect of a mail API.

> If communication and payment diverge, the operator invents a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet always wins.

## Quiet UI, loud machine

The interface is monochrome and sparse on purpose. Operators do not need a celebration when an invoice is paid. They need to trust the row. The work went into the projector that makes that row true.

- Never let the client confirm money.
- Reminders read current state; they do not assume the past.
- A human should be able to explain an invoice’s history from events alone.
